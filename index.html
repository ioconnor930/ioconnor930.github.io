About

I work on how computing hardware can keep getting more energy-efficient as transistor scaling slows: by building circuits and architectures from emerging devices, by moving data with light instead of copper, and by developing the design methods that let these heterogeneous technologies be explored together.

I am Distinguished Professor at Ecole Centrale de Lyon, joint head of the Electronics group at the Lyon Institute of Nanotechnology, and Director of the CNRS GdR SoC2 research network on systems-on-chip, embedded systems and IoT. I co-direct PHOENIX, a France 2030 programme establishing a national hub for advanced integrated circuit design. From 2008 to 2022 I was also Adjunct Professor at Ecole Polytechnique de Montréal.

I serve the design-automation community as Vice-President for Publications of the IEEE Council on Electronic Design Automation (CEDA), Chair of the Main Board of the European Design Automation Association (EDAA), and Area Editor for IEEE Transactions on Computer-Aided Design of Integrated Circuits and Systems. I was General Chair of DATE 2023. I am also an expert with Collège Numérique France 2030, the IRDS Systems and Architectures focus team, and IFIP WG 10.5.

Before joining Centrale Lyon in 1998, I was a Senior CAD Engineer at Philips (now NXP) Semiconductors in Southampton. I studied in the UK, France and Germany (BEng and MSc, University of Essex / ESIEE / Universität Karlsruhe), received my PhD from the University of Lille in 1997 and my Habilitation from Centrale Lyon in 2005.

Research

Three connected lines of work, each going from device to system. Each is carried out with device and technology partners in France and abroad, from CEA-LETI and NaMLab to IMEC, RMIT and Polytechnique Montréal.

Energy-efficient edge computing with emerging devices

Edge AI must run close to the sensor on a tight energy budget. Emerging devices offer new building blocks for this: vertical nanowire FETs that favour regular 3D logic structures, HfO₂-based ferroelectric capacitors and FeFETs for fine-grain logic-in-memory and “normally-off” computing, reconfigurable FETs, and 2D van der Waals ferroelectrics. My group designs the logic cells, memories and in-memory computing architectures that exploit them, and assesses what they bring at system level.

  • Ultra-fine-grain reconfigurable cells on ambipolar double-gate CNTFETs and DG-MOSFETs (patented), with matching synthesis and mapping flows
  • 3D logic circuits and a 3D neural-network compute cube on vertical nanowire FETs (FVLLMONTI)
  • FeFET and FeMFET memories and in-memory accelerators for edge AI (3eFerro, Ferro4EdgeAI, FerroFutures)
  • VNWFET
  • FeFET / FeMFET
  • RFET
  • Logic-in-memory
  • Edge AI

Silicon photonics for high-performance and in-network computing

Manycore and AI systems need aggregate bandwidths beyond 100 TB/s, which metallic interconnect cannot deliver efficiently. We were among the first to quantify the performance of on-chip optical interconnect per technology node, and have since developed wavelength-routed optical network-on-chip topologies, system-level models and thermal-aware laser control. Current work targets broadcast-aware nanophotonic interconnect below 0.1 pJ/bit, and photonic computing: matrix multiplication with phase-change materials and stochastic computing, reservoir computing, and photonic convolutional neural networks on lithium niobate.

  • Optical networks-on-chip and their interface to industrial communication protocols
  • Photonic GEMM based on phase-change materials and stochastic computing (Octane)
  • Secure neuromorphic photonic accelerators (Neuropuls) and event-driven photonic circuit simulation
  • Optical NoC
  • PCM photonics
  • LNOI
  • Photonic neural networks

Design–technology co-optimization for heterogeneous systems

More-than-Moore and 3D integration bring sensors, analog, photonic and digital functions into the same package or die, but design technology has not kept pace. This line of work develops methods to predictively extract and exploit design information across physical domains and abstraction levels, and now extends to system–technology co-optimization of chiplet-based and near-memory architectures.

  • Predictive synthesis of multi-physics systems through hierarchical combination of Pareto fronts (patented)
  • Thermal- and cost-aware floorplanning of 3D ICs; analog variability bounds via the Cornish–Fisher expansion
  • DTCO/STCO methodologies for emerging memories and near-memory computing (CHOOSE, PHOENIX)
  • DTCO / STCO
  • 3D integration
  • Chiplets
  • Multi-physics synthesis
