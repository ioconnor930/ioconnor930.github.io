The Co-Optimized Silicon Stack

A series of posts, published weekly on LinkedIn from August 2026, on how chip technology may evolve over the next decade and how research, industrial capacity, institutions and policy shape who can build what. The common thread: no single device, EDA, packaging or manufacturing breakthrough will decide who leads, and the hardest task is to specify the interfaces between disciplines as rigorously as the disciplines themselves.

  1. 01 25 August 2026

    Co-Optimizing the Silicon Stack

    Why chip leadership over the next decade may depend less on the smallest transistor than on co-optimizing a stack that spans devices, EDA, packaging, manufacturing and policy. The introduction to the series.

    Read on LinkedIn

  2. 02 1 September 2026

    Trillion-Transistor Packaging

    Trillion-transistor AI processors will come from 3D chiplet assemblies, hybrid bonding and UCIe links. The hard problems move to thermal coupling, power delivery, test and known-good-die validation.

    Read on LinkedIn

  3. 03 4 September 2026

    Advanced Devices for the Next Dimension

    From gate-all-around nanosheets to forksheets, CFETs, 2D materials and reconfigurable FETs: each new device adds coupling that design flows must model, so it has to arrive with models, libraries and sign-off.

    Read on LinkedIn

  4. 04 9 September 2026

    Silicon Photonics and Co-Packaged Optics

    Co-packaged optics moves the optical engine into the package. Optics can supply the bandwidth; making it scale is now a question of multiphysics design, assembly yield and sign-off.

    Read on LinkedIn

  5. 05 16 September 2026

    Connectivity: 6G and Satellite Chips

    Very large antenna arrays in the 7–15 GHz band and satellite links force the whole radio chipset, from data converters to beamformers and power amplifiers, to be redesigned together.

    Read on LinkedIn

  6. 06 22 September 2026

    Robotics and Physical AI Silicon

    Physical AI needs low-latency inference, sensor fusion and motor control on one die within a battery power budget, which pulls robotics silicon away from data-centre accelerator design.

    Read on LinkedIn

  7. 07 29 September 2026

    Talent Shortages and Career Appeal

    The chip-design skills gap is not only a recruitment problem: tools and curricula are built around disciplinary boundaries that today’s hardest design problems no longer respect.

    Read on LinkedIn

  8. 08 10 October 2026

    Memory as an Architectural Resource

    CXL turns memory from a hidden hierarchy into a pooled, shared resource. Coherence, workload-aware allocation and fault containment become architectural questions.

    Read on LinkedIn

  9. 09 19 October 2026

    Chiplet Reuse as Real IP

    A die that complies with UCIe is not yet reusable IP. A chiplet economy needs portable, machine-readable models of thermal, power, test, security and reliability behaviour.

    Read on LinkedIn

  10. 10 26 October 2026

    Where is Open-Source Hardware Going?

    Open-source EDA flows and PDKs are now mature on established nodes. The interesting question is where the line of what remains commercially gated moves next, and who moves it.

    Read on LinkedIn

  11. 11 2 November 2026

    The Sustainability of Scaling

    Each advanced node and 3D step adds energy, water and emissions per wafer. The embodied cost of scaling is becoming a constraint the industry can no longer simply optimize around.

    Read on LinkedIn

  12. 12 9 November 2026

    From PPA to PPV

    In 3D systems, how much heat can leave each cubic millimetre limits a design more than cell area. Power, performance and volume should replace power, performance and area as the guiding metric.

    Read on LinkedIn
